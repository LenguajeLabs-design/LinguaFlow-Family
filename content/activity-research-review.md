## Batch 3 editorial assessment — ages 12–14

### Strongest candidates

- `Practice the Ask`: useful, immediately actionable self-advocacy with a clear home-to-school connection.
- `Compare the Coverage`: authentic bilingual media literacy, especially when one source is in the home language.
- `Teach Me the Rules`: puts the teenager in the expert role and creates rich explanation without feeling instructional.
- `Plan the Trip`: real responsibility, meaningful language, and a natural reason to compare options and trade-offs.
- `Draft It Out Loud`: a practical bridge from oral language to writing that can help without a parent taking over the work.
- `Family Language Legacy`: the strongest identity and intergenerational activity in the batch.

### Revisions to request before publication

- Rename `Accent Pride Check-in` more broadly, such as `Language Confidence Check-in`. The current activity is valuable, but the title assumes pride and may make a vulnerable teen feel that there is a preferred emotional response.
- Add an explicit opt-out to the language-confidence activity. Parents should invite the conversation once, listen, and avoid pressing if their teenager does not want to talk.
- For `Debate at the Table`, add a rule that either person may veto a topic and that the goal is understanding a position, not winning.
- For `Compare the Coverage`, provide guidance for selecting credible, age-appropriate sources and distinguish differences in perspective from misinformation.
- For `Mock Interview Minute`, remove the timer from the starting version. A sixty-second constraint may increase performance anxiety before confidence has been established.
- For `Summarize the Article`, avoid calling it one of the most powerful comprehension skills unless the final source directly substantiates that exact comparative claim.
- For activities involving schoolwork, state that the parent prompts thinking but does not correct, grade, rewrite, or complete the task.

### Evidence notes

- The classifications are more appropriately conservative than in earlier batches: nine of ten are framed as adaptations or practitioner recommendations.
- `Summarize the Article` should still be reviewed carefully. Research strongly supports explicit summarization instruction, but this exact two-sentence parent routine was not directly tested by the cited sources; `Adapted from a research-supported principle` is the safer classification.
- `Accent Pride Check-in`, `Compare the Coverage`, `Teach Me the Rules`, `Plan the Trip`, and `Mock Interview Minute` currently rely on broad principles or secondary practitioner sources. Their parent-facing claims should remain modest.
- `Draft It Out Loud` extrapolates from research with younger children. Its current classification as an adaptation is appropriate, but the wording should not imply direct evidence for adolescents.
- Replace every `<cite index="…">` marker before the content enters the product or newsletter.

## Recommendation for the public library

Do not publish all 30 activities as a flat catalogue. Wait for the final Strongest 12, then use those as a curated starting collection with age, time, and goal filters. Keep the remaining activities as an expandable research-backed content reserve for newsletters and future recommendations.

# LinguaFlow Family — Reading and Writing Pathway Brief

**Status:** Editorial and implementation brief  
**Source:** Parent mission note and Claude's 24-strategy reading/writing draft, reviewed August 2026

## North star

LinguaFlow Family reduces the distance between families and useful knowledge. It connects research to teachers, teachers to parents, parents to students, and AI-supported work to real human relationships.

Every parent-facing decision should pass one test:

> Does a parent leave knowing what to do next—and feeling capable of doing it?

The product should organize around the questions families bring, not around the resources the site happens to contain. Parents are learning partners. They are asked to listen, ask, and encourage—not grade, correct, or complete schoolwork.

## Recommended next public experience

Add two plain-language routes within Family Learning Activities:

- **Help with Reading** — “Simple ways to help your child understand, enjoy, and talk about what they read.”
- **Help with Writing** — “Simple ways to help your child develop ideas and express what they mean.”

Do not present a 24-card database. Start from a parent need, then age, then offer no more than three useful choices.

Suggested entry questions:

- I have 10 minutes. What can we do?
- My child does not enjoy reading.
- My child gets stuck when writing.
- I do not speak much English.
- Show me ideas for my child’s age.

## Launch set

Use 12 strategies: two reading and two writing strategies for each age band.

| Age | Reading | Writing |
|---|---|---|
| 6–8 | Picture Walk Before Reading; Retell with Toys | Draw and Label; Say It Before You Write It |
| 9–11 | Pause and Wonder; Home-Language Debrief | Messy Draft Permission; Read It to a Listener |
| 12–14 | Debate a Claim; Book vs. Screen | Real-Life Opinions; Say More Prompts |

The existing **Picture Walk Before Reading** activity can become the first complete pathway. It should not be duplicated as a new card.

## Card experience

The first view of a strategy should contain only:

1. A parent-centered promise
2. Age and time
3. Materials
4. Three short steps
5. One phrase the parent can say
6. A visible home-language option
7. **What success can look like**

Keep these details expandable:

- Make it easier
- Make it more challenging
- Why it helps
- Research and sources
- Cautions

Use this reassurance consistently:

> You are doing enough if you spend a few minutes listening and talking with your child.

Success should describe connection, not task completion. Examples include a child sharing one more idea, asking a question, smiling, or wanting to continue later.

## First pathway to build and test

**Today or newsletter link → Help with Reading → Ages 6–8 → Picture Walk Before Reading**

On a phone, a parent should be able to:

- understand the purpose within five seconds;
- reach the activity in two taps;
- see that any home language is welcome before scrolling;
- begin without opening research details; and
- finish knowing they did enough.

Only after this works should the structure be repeated for the other 11 strategies.

## Eight-week newsletter sequence

1. **Your language is an asset** — introduce “listen, ask, encourage.”
2. **Pictures before words** — Picture Walk + Draw and Label.
3. **Play and talk first** — Retell with Toys + Say It Before You Write It.
4. **Let go of the red pen** — Messy Draft Permission + Pause and Wonder.
5. **Your language supports comprehension** — Home-Language Debrief + Read It to a Listener.
6. **Real opinions, real reasons** — Debate a Claim + Real-Life Opinions.
7. **Say more, do not fix** — Say More Prompts + Book vs. Screen.
8. **What should come next?** — recap and invite family feedback.

Each newsletter should have one primary action and one direct activity URL. It should not ask families to browse the whole site.

## Editorial review required before publication

The strategy concepts and parent-facing tone are strong, but the supplied research draft is **not publication-ready** because its citations were lost or malformed. Many source fields appear as empty Markdown links such as `]()` and the final source list is incomplete.

Before displaying evidence labels publicly:

1. Recover the exact title, author or organization, publication date, and URL for every cited source.
2. Verify that each source supports the specific claim attached to it.
3. Distinguish clearly between direct research support and an adaptation of a supported principle.
4. Prefer primary research, What Works Clearinghouse guidance, WIDA, and established literacy organizations.
5. Have a literacy or EAL educator review the final wording.
6. Have Chinese and Korean translations reviewed by native speakers rather than relying on machine translation alone.

Until that review is complete, omit public evidence labels rather than publishing unsupported authority signals.

## Product boundaries for this phase

Do not add accounts, AI recommendations, progress tracking, broad search, complex stage filters, or all 24 strategies yet. The milestone is smaller:

> A busy parent can open LinguaFlow Family on a phone and find one useful reading or writing action in under 30 seconds.

## Feedback loop

After an activity, ask no more than two optional questions:

- Was this easy to try? Yes / A little / No
- Would you like more ideas like this? Yes / Maybe / No

Avoid asking about the child’s name, assessment results, or other identifying information. Decide where responses will be stored and publish a clear privacy explanation before collecting them.
